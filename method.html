The method

Two ratings are meaningless side by side. Put each one in its own league first, and the comparison becomes honest.

01The two ranks

Every book is ranked against books, and every film against films. A film in the 91st percentile is rated above 91% of films; a book in the 88th is above 88% of books.

Ranks are shrunk toward their baseline mean to keep small sample sizes from shouting, so a title with forty votes cannot outrank one with forty thousand.

02The gap

Subtract the film's percentile from the book's. A positive number means the book sits higher in its own medium; a negative number means the film does. Two points either way is a dead heat.

050100th percentile

A book in the 74th and a film in the 87th: the film ranks 13 points higher. The scale is percentile, not a star rating — the dots move within their own leagues.

03The other numbers

Page to screen is the wait between publication and release, in years. Pages per film minute is the compression: how much prose each minute of film has to carry. Box office multiple is gross divided by production budget, inflation-adjusted.

None of these decide the verdict. They explain it.

04What this is not

It is not a review, and it is not a claim that one version is better art. It compares two crowds: the people who rate books, and the people who rate films. Where those crowds disagree, the gap is real even when the argument is not.

Books flagged low reliability are shown, but marked, because a thin sample can still rise.

05The match

Each book is linked to a film by title, author, year and runtime evidence. Every link carries a confidence score; anything below the threshold is held for review rather than published. A case like Dune 1965 to Dune 2021 sits at 98%.

Sources: OpenLibrary for books, TMDB for films. Percentiles and divergence are recomputed on each snapshot.
